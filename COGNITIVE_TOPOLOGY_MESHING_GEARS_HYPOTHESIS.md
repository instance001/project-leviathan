# Project Leviathan — Cognitive Topology and the Meshing Gears Hypothesis

## Status

Exploratory architecture / research hypothesis.

This note connects three emerging Leviathan research lanes:

- cognitive cartography;
- relational curriculum geometry;
- the meshing-gears model of cognition.

The purpose is not to claim that language models contain literal gears or that biological cognition is mechanically identical to a clockwork device.

The claim is narrower:

> If learned relational structures differ in how easily they can transfer useful structure into one another, then their compatibility can be measured behaviorally and represented as a topology of cognitive engagement.

The gear metaphor is a mechanistic abstraction for that measured compatibility.

---

## Core hypothesis

A learned knowledge region is not useful only because of what it contains.

It may also differ in:

- what other regions it can connect to;
- how easily those connections occur;
- whether transfer is directional;
- how much scaffolding is required;
- whether useful structure survives the transition;
- how far a valid derivation can propagate;
- whether the connection produces insight or unsupported analogy.

This suggests that cognition-like performance may depend partly on the shape and compatibility of learned relational structures.

In mechanical terms:

> Some cognitive structures mesh readily.  
> Some mesh poorly.  
> Some require intermediate gears.  
> Some slip.  
> Some engage only under particular conditions.  
> Some can transmit useful motion across long chains.

The research task is to determine whether measurable behavior supports this abstraction.

---

## From metaphor to measurement

The meshing-gears idea becomes experimentally useful only when its terms can be connected to observable quantities.

The proposed mapping is:

| Mechanical abstraction | Cognitive-topology measurement |
|---|---|
| Gear engagement | Successful relational transfer between regions or domains |
| Tooth compatibility | Relational compatibility between learned structures |
| Gear ratio / transmission | Change in derivational reach produced by another region |
| Bridge cost | Amount of reasoning, context, or scaffolding required for transfer |
| Intermediate gear | Additional concept, representation, or domain required to enable transfer |
| Direction of drive | Asymmetric assistance between domains |
| Slippage | Unsupported analogy, false bridge, or high-confidence invalid transfer |
| Propagation | Successive earned derivations transmitted through a chain |
| Compound gearing | Multiple domains jointly enabling a result unavailable to either alone |
| Jam / dead end | Valid reasoning path that cannot progress further |
| Gearbox selection | Host-mediated routing toward a more compatible relational path |

The metaphor is therefore downstream of the measurements.

The measurements do not exist to prove the metaphor.

The metaphor exists to describe a recurring functional pattern if the measurements support it.

---

## The withheld-C primitive

The key experimental primitive is:

**A + B, with C withheld → attempt to derive C**

Before the derivation test, the model must demonstrate competence in the relevant domain.

This allows a distinction between:

- knowledge already present;
- novel derivation;
- unresolved relational gaps;
- unsupported bridges;
- failures caused by being outside the model's competence envelope.

Repeated across many starting points, distances, and domains, these tests can produce a cognitive topology.

That topology provides the empirical substrate for the meshing-gears hypothesis.

---

## What counts as a good mesh?

Suppose a model is competent in domains X and Y.

If knowledge from Y reliably improves the model's ability to derive withheld conclusions in X, then Y has a measurable enabling relationship with X.

A strong mesh may show:

- higher verified C-arrival rate;
- lower scaffolding requirement;
- longer cumulative derivation chains;
- lower false-bridge rate;
- greater resilience to representation change;
- better transfer across relational distance.

A weak mesh may show:

- inconsistent transfer;
- high scaffold cost;
- frequent unresolved gaps;
- brittle dependence on wording;
- rapid loss of evidence support.

A false mesh may look superficially productive while increasing unsupported analogy or hallucination.

---

## Directionality

Meshing should not be assumed to be symmetric.

Example:

**Algebra → Chemistry**

may strongly improve some chemistry derivations because algebra supplies useful machinery for:

- proportionality;
- equivalence;
- variable manipulation;
- transformation;
- conservation;
- rates.

But:

**Chemistry → Algebra**

may provide little corresponding assistance.

The topology should therefore be represented as a directed graph.

A directed edge means:

> Learned structure in region Y measurably changes the reachability of novel conclusions in region X.

The edge can carry weights for:

- strength;
- reliability;
- cost;
- depth;
- uncertainty;
- false-transfer rate.

---

## Intermediate gears

Some cognitive regions may not connect cleanly on their own.

For example:

**X → Z**

may be weak or unavailable.

But:

**X → Y → Z**

may become reliable if Y provides an intermediate representation or relational structure.

This is the cognitive equivalent of inserting an intermediate gear.

The intermediate may be:

- an auxiliary domain;
- a formal representation;
- a causal decomposition;
- an analogy;
- a symbolic transformation;
- a spatial model;
- a host-generated scaffold.

This creates a testable question:

> Can previously weak or unavailable relational transfer be enabled by inserting a specific intermediate structure?

If yes, Leviathan can begin learning not merely where connections exist, but what kinds of intermediary make them possible.

---

## Compound gearing

Some derivations may require combinations of learned structures.

Possible pattern:

**X + Y → little improvement**  
**X + Z → little improvement**  
**X + Y + Z → large improvement**

This suggests a higher-order interaction.

The relevant cognitive mechanism may not belong to any one domain.

It may emerge only when multiple relational structures are simultaneously available.

Such cases should be treated as compound gearing rather than simple pairwise assistance.

---

## Slippage and false engagement

A connection that feels semantically plausible is not necessarily a valid mesh.

Models may produce:

- superficial analogies;
- category errors;
- unsupported transfers;
- persuasive but false causal bridges;
- conclusions that resemble C without being evidentially reachable.

These are the equivalent of mechanical slippage.

A useful map must therefore distinguish:

> successful engagement

from:

> apparent engagement that does not transmit grounded structure.

Verification is essential.

Without it, the map would reward confident bullshit as cognitive connectivity.

---

## Propagation and earned novelty

The strongest evidence for a working cognitive gear train is not a single successful derivation.

It is propagation.

If:

**A + B → C**

and C is independently verified, then C can become new grounded territory:

**C + D → E**

then:

**E + F → G**

This creates a chain of earned novelty.

The resulting questions include:

- How many valid hops can be sustained?
- Where does error accumulation begin?
- Which starting regions produce long productive chains?
- Which conclusions are fertile?
- Which conclusions are terminal?
- Which domain combinations transmit structure furthest?

This is closer to a functioning gear train than a one-shot benchmark.

---

## Relational Curriculum Geometry

The meshing hypothesis becomes more powerful if topology can be altered experimentally.

Relational Curriculum Geometry proposes that training order, relational organization, and curriculum structure may change how learned material is arranged.

If two models receive similar factual content but different curriculum geometry, then their cognitive maps may differ.

The causal chain under test becomes:

**curriculum geometry**  
↓  
**learned relational topology**  
↓  
**different meshing relationships**  
↓  
**different reachability of withheld C**  
↓  
**different system-level reasoning behavior**

This gives a concrete experimental path from corpus design to observed cognitive performance.

---

## A strong test of the hypothesis

A useful experiment would hold model architecture, token budget, and target-domain factual competence approximately constant while changing corpus organization.

If the resulting models show different:

- bridge strengths;
- launch-point quality;
- cumulative depth;
- cross-domain assistance;
- hallucination cliffs;
- scaffold requirements;

then the result would suggest that relational organization matters independently of simple knowledge quantity.

An even stronger result would be the ability to predict a topology change from a curriculum intervention and then observe that change.

For example:

> A curriculum designed to strengthen algebraic transformation as a reusable relational tool should increase verified algebra → chemistry assistance on a predefined set of withheld-C tasks.

Prediction before measurement matters.

---

## Leviathan as survey rig and gearbox

Cognitive cartography supplies the map.

The meshing-gears model supplies a mechanistic abstraction.

Leviathan can then use the map operationally.

The host may learn that:

- two regions mesh directly;
- two regions require an intermediate;
- a particular path frequently slips;
- a particular domain is a productive launch point;
- a certain representation reduces bridge cost;
- another route produces unsupported confidence.

The host can then route accordingly.

This does not mean the host decides truth by prior preference.

It means prior topology can guide which candidate path is tried first while verification continues to determine what survives.

A future Leviathan system could therefore act like a gearbox:

> select a promising relational path,  
> engage it,  
> verify transmission,  
> detect slippage,  
> insert intermediates when necessary,  
> and preserve the resulting evidence.

---

## Important restraint

The meshing-gears hypothesis must not become an ontology imposed on the data.

The system should remain capable of discovering that:

- some domains do not behave like distinct gears at all;
- boundaries are fuzzy;
- compatibility changes by task;
- topology is dynamic rather than fixed;
- model architecture matters more than corpus geometry in some regions;
- the metaphor fails in particular cases.

If the measured behavior does not support the mechanical abstraction, the abstraction should be revised.

The metaphor is a tool, not a protected belief.

---

## Research program

The proposed sequence is:

1. Build controlled base corpora.
2. Demonstrate target-domain competence.
3. Withhold C.
4. Measure C reachability from A+B.
5. Vary relational distance.
6. Repeat from many starting points.
7. Measure within-domain threadhopping.
8. Add auxiliary domains.
9. Measure directed cross-domain assistance.
10. Test intermediate and compound relationships.
11. Track hallucination cliffs and false bridges.
12. Promote verified C into further tests of earned novelty.
13. Compare different corpus geometries.
14. Compare different model architectures.
15. Compare raw model behavior with Leviathan host scaffolding.
16. Aggregate results into cognitive topology maps.

---

## Working interpretation

If the hypothesis survives testing, the result would support the following interpretation:

> Learned cognition can be studied as interacting relational structures whose ability to engage, transfer, and propagate useful information varies measurably.

In that framework:

- cognitive cartography measures the terrain;
- withheld-C tests provide the probe;
- cross-domain affordance mapping measures connection strength;
- Relational Curriculum Geometry alters the terrain;
- meshing gears provide the mechanistic abstraction;
- Leviathan supplies the host machinery for routing, verification, and cumulative propagation.

---

## Core statement

The hypothesis can be reduced to one sentence:

> The shape of learned knowledge may determine not only what a model knows, but what other knowledge it can successfully engage with to produce further thought.

That is the bridge between cognitive topology and the meshing-gears model.
